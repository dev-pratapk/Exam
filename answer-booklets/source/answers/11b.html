Problem

Given: F(P, Q, R) = (¬P → R) ∧ (Q ↔ P).

To find: the principal disjunctive normal form (PDNF) and the principal conjunctive normal form (PCNF) of F.

Step 1: Eliminate → and ↔

¬P → R ≡ ¬(¬P) ∨ R ≡ P ∨ R (implication law)
Q ↔ P ≡ (Q → P) ∧ (P → Q) ≡ (¬Q ∨ P) ∧ (¬P ∨ Q)

Hence

F ≡ (P ∨ R) ∧ (¬Q ∨ P) ∧ (¬P ∨ Q).

Step 3: Principal disjunctive normal form

F is true (F = 1) exactly in the rows 001, 110 and 111. Taking the product of the variables in each true row, negating a variable when its value is 0,

F ≡ (¬P ∧ ¬Q ∧ R) ∨ (P ∧ Q ∧ ¬R) ∨ (P ∧ Q ∧ R)
F ≡ Σm(1, 6, 7).

This is the required PDNF: a disjunction of the three minterms corresponding to the true rows of F.

Step 4: Principal conjunctive normal form

F is false (F = 0) in the rows 000, 010, 011, 100 and 101. The maxterm of a row is the disjunction of the variables, negating a variable when its value is 1. Thus the five maxterms are (P ∨ Q ∨ R), (P ∨ ¬Q ∨ R), (P ∨ ¬Q ∨ ¬R), (¬P ∨ Q ∨ R) and (¬P ∨ Q ∨ ¬R). Therefore

F ≡ (P ∨ Q ∨ R) ∧ (P ∨ ¬Q ∨ R) ∧ (P ∨ ¬Q ∨ ¬R) ∧ (¬P ∨ Q ∨ R) ∧ (¬P ∨ Q ∨ ¬R)
F ≡ ΠM(0, 2, 3, 4, 5).

This is the required PCNF: a conjunction of the five maxterms corresponding to the false rows of F.

PDNF: F ≡ (¬P ∧ ¬Q ∧ R) ∨ (P ∧ Q ∧ ¬R) ∨ (P ∧ Q ∧ R) ≡ Σm(1, 6, 7)
PCNF: F ≡ (P ∨ Q ∨ R) ∧ (P ∨ ¬Q ∨ R) ∧ (P ∨ ¬Q ∨ ¬R) ∧ (¬P ∨ Q ∨ R) ∧ (¬P ∨ Q ∨ ¬R) ≡ ΠM(0, 2, 3, 4, 5)

Check. The PDNF uses the 3 true rows and the PCNF the 5 false rows of the 8 possible rows, and 3 + 5 = 8; also the same function gives both forms.

Problem

Given: the premise (∀x)(P(x) ∨ Q(x)).

To prove: (∀x)P(x) ∨ (∃x)Q(x).

Method: Indirect method (proof by contradiction)

In the indirect method we assume the premise to be true and the conclusion to be false, and derive a contradiction. The assumption that the conclusion is false is

¬[(∀x)P(x) ∨ (∃x)Q(x)] ≡ ¬(∀x)P(x) ∧ ¬(∃x)Q(x) ≡ (∃x)¬P(x) ∧ (∀x)¬Q(x),

using De Morgan's law and the quantified negations ¬(∀x)A(x) ≡ (∃x)¬A(x), ¬(∃x)A(x) ≡ (∀x)¬A(x).

Proof

2¬[(∀x)P(x) ∨ (∃x)Q(x)]Rule P (negation of the conclusion, assumed for indirect proof)
3(∃x)¬P(x) ∧ (∀x)¬Q(x)Rule T on (2), using De Morgan's law and quantified negation
4(∀x)¬Q(x)Rule T (Simplification) on (3)
5(∃x)¬P(x)Rule T (Simplification) on (3)
6¬P(a)Rule ES on (5); a denotes the fixed element guaranteed by (5)
7P(a) ∨ Q(a)Rule US on (1)
8¬Q(a)Rule US on (4)
9P(a)Rule T (Disjunctive syllogism) on (7) and (8)
10P(a) ∧ ¬P(a)Rule T (Conjunction) on (9) and (6): a contradiction

Conclusion of the indirect proof. Step (10) is a contradiction (P(a) and ¬P(a) cannot both hold). Therefore our additional assumption (2), namely that the conclusion is false, must be wrong. Hence the conclusion cannot be false whenever the premise is true.

Hence (∀x)(P(x) ∨ Q(x)) ⇒ (∀x)P(x) ∨ (∃x)Q(x). ∎
