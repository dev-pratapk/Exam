Statement

If N1 and N2 are two normal subgroups of a group ⟨G, *⟩, then their intersection N1 ∩ N2 is also a normal subgroup of G.

Given and to prove

Given: N1 and N2 are normal subgroups of G; that is, each is a subgroup of G and g * x * g−1 ∈ Ni whenever x ∈ Ni, g ∈ G. Let N = N1 ∩ N2. To prove: N is a normal subgroup of G.

Definitions used

  • Subgroup: a non-empty subset H of G such that a, b ∈ H ⇒ a * b−1 ∈ H.
  • Normal subgroup: a subgroup N of G such that g * n * g−1 ∈ N for all n ∈ N and all g ∈ G (equivalently, gN = Ng for all g ∈ G).

Part 1: N is a subgroup of G

  1. N is non-empty. Since N1 and N2 are subgroups, the identity e ∈ N1 and e ∈ N2. Hence e ∈ N1 ∩ N2 = N, so N ≠ ∅.
  2. One-step subgroup test. Let a, b ∈ N. Then a, b ∈ N1 and a, b ∈ N2. Since N1 is a subgroup, a * b−1 ∈ N1; since N2 is a subgroup, a * b−1 ∈ N2. Therefore
    a * b−1 ∈ N1 ∩ N2 = N.

Thus N is a non-empty subset of G closed under the operation and under inverses. Hence N is a subgroup of G.

Part 2: N is normal in G

  1. Let x ∈ N and g ∈ G be arbitrary. Since x ∈ N = N1 ∩ N2, we have x ∈ N1 and x ∈ N2.
  2. Because N1 is normal in G and x ∈ N1,
    g * x * g−1 ∈ N1.
  3. Because N2 is normal in G and x ∈ N2,
    g * x * g−1 ∈ N2.
  4. From steps 2 and 3, g * x * g−1 belongs to both N1 and N2, so
    g * x * g−1 ∈ N1 ∩ N2 = N.

As this holds for every x ∈ N and every g ∈ G, N is a normal subgroup of G.

Remark (extension)

The same argument shows that the intersection of any family of normal subgroups of G is a normal subgroup of G, and more generally the intersection of any family of subgroups of G is a subgroup of G.

Hence N1 ∩ N2 is a normal subgroup of ⟨G, *⟩. ∎

Statement

Every subgroup of a cyclic group is cyclic.

Given and to prove

Given: ⟨G, *⟩ is a cyclic group generated by a, so G = ⟨a⟩ = {an : n ∈ ℤ}, and H is a subgroup of G. To prove: H is cyclic, that is, H = ⟨b⟩ for some b ∈ H.

Definitions used

  • Cyclic group: a group G is cyclic if G = ⟨a⟩ = {an : n ∈ ℤ} for some a ∈ G; a is called a generator.
  • Subgroup generated by b: ⟨b⟩ = {bn : n ∈ ℤ}, the smallest subgroup of G containing b.
  • Well-ordering principle: every non-empty set of positive integers has a least element.

Proof

Since H ≤ G = ⟨a⟩, every element of H is of the form ak for some integer k. We consider two cases.

Case 1: H = {e}

If H consists of the identity alone, then H = ⟨e⟩, since ⟨e⟩ = {en : n ∈ ℤ} = {e}. Hence H is cyclic, generated by e (= a0).

Case 2: H ≠ {e}

  1. H contains a positive power of a. Choose x ∈ H with x ≠ e. Since x ∈ G = ⟨a⟩, write x = ak with k ∈ ℤ, k ≠ 0. If k < 0 then, because H is a subgroup, x−1 = a−k ∈ H and −k > 0. So H contains am for some positive integer m.
  2. Choose the least positive exponent. The set
    S = { m ∈ ℤ+ : am ∈ H }
    is a non-empty set of positive integers, so by the well-ordering principle it has a least element; call it m. Then am ∈ H, and no smaller positive power of a lies in H.
  3. Claim: H = ⟨am⟩. We prove both inclusions.
  4. ⟨am⟩ ⊆ H. Since am ∈ H and H is closed under the operation and inverses, every integral power (am)q = amq (q ∈ ℤ) belongs to H. Hence ⟨am⟩ ⊆ H.
  5. H ⊆ ⟨am⟩. Let y ∈ H. Since H ⊆ G = ⟨a⟩, write y = ak with k ∈ ℤ. By the division algorithm, divide k by m:
    k = qm + r,   q, r ∈ ℤ,   0 ≤ r < m.
    Then
    ar = ak−qm = ak * (am)−q.
    Now ak = y ∈ H, and (am)−q ∈ H by step 4, so ar ∈ H (H is closed).
  6. The remainder must be zero. If 0 < r < m, then ar ∈ H with r a positive integer smaller than m, contradicting the minimality of m. Hence r = 0, so k = qm and
    y = ak = aqm = (am)q ∈ ⟨am⟩.
    Therefore H ⊆ ⟨am⟩.

From steps 4 and 6, H = ⟨am⟩, so H is generated by the single element am and is therefore cyclic.

Illustration

In the cyclic group ℤ12 = ⟨1⟩ (addition mod 12), the subgroup generated by 4 is H = {0, 4, 8}; taking m = 4, indeed H = ⟨4⟩, a cyclic subgroup of order 3, and 3 divides 12 = |ℤ12|, as Lagrange's theorem predicts.

Hence every subgroup H of a cyclic group G = ⟨a⟩ is cyclic; in fact H = ⟨am⟩, where m is the least positive integer with am ∈ H. ∎
