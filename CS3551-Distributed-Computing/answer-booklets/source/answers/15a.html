Introduction

The NIST definition: cloud computing is a model for enabling ubiquitous, convenient, on-demand network access to a shared pool of configurable computing resources (networks, servers, storage, applications and services) that can be rapidly provisioned and released with minimal management effort or service-provider interaction. A deployment model says who owns the cloud, who can use it and where it is located. There are four types: public, private, community and hybrid.

1. Public cloud

The infrastructure is owned and operated by a cloud provider and made available to the general public or a large industry group. Many customers (tenants) share the same physical resources over the Internet and pay per use. Examples: Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud Platform.

No capital expenditure; pay-as-you-go

2. Private cloud

The infrastructure is provisioned for exclusive use by a single organization. It may be owned, managed and operated by the organization, by a third party, or both, and may be located on-premise or off-premise. Examples: a data centre running OpenStack or VMware vSphere.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Highest security and control
Easier compliance with regulations
Customization to own needs

Needs skilled staff
Resources may stay under-used

3. Community cloud

The infrastructure is shared by several organizations with common concerns (mission, security requirements, policy, compliance). It may be managed by the organizations or by a third party. Examples: a government cloud shared by departments, or a cloud shared by hospitals or universities.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Cost is shared among members
Common security and compliance requirements
More secure than a public cloud		Limited capacity and scalability
Governance and cost-sharing among members is complex
Fewer providers and choices

4. Hybrid cloud

A composition of two or more distinct clouds (private, community or public) that remain separate entities but are bound together by standardized technology that enables data and application portability. A typical use is cloud bursting: the normal load runs on the private cloud and the peak load overflows to the public cloud.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Sensitive data stays private while the public cloud supplies elasticity
Cost optimization and flexibility

Integration, networking and security between the clouds

Choosing a model

Choose public for low cost and fast scaling of non-sensitive workloads; private when security, regulation or control dominate; community when organizations share the same requirements and want to share cost; hybrid when the workload has both sensitive and bursty parts.

Hence the four deployment models (public, private, community and hybrid) trade off cost, control, security and scalability, and the choice depends on the workload and the organization’s requirements.
